# Fashion Sales Data Analysis

## Project Overview

This project involves analysing fashion sales data using Python. The aim is to understand how a fashion business is performing by looking at its sales, product, categories, discounts, gender, size, color, sales channel and seasonal trends. The dataset was generated by ChatGPT.

The project covers data profiling, data cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory data analysis, and data visualisation. The analysis is used to identify patterns in the dataset and understand how the results can help a fashion business make better decisions.

## Business Case

Fashion businesses deal with different products, changing customer preferences, and variations in demand. To run their businesses effectively, they need to understand which products sell well, when sales are higher, and how season, pricing and discounts affect sales.

Analysing sales data can help a business understand these patterns and use the information to improve its sales strategies, manage inventory, and plan for different seasons.

This project applies data analysis techniques to fashion sales data to gain a better understanding of sales performance and identify areas where the business can improve.

## Feature Engineering

Feature engineering was carried out to create additional columns from the existing data. These new columns made it possible to examine sales performance in more detail.

The following variables were created:

**Gross Sales:** This represents the total value of products before discounts. It was calculated by multiplying the quantity sold by the unit price.

`gross_sales = quantity * unit_price`

**Discount Amount:** This represents the amount deducted from the gross sales based on the discount applied. The calculation depends on whether discounts are stored as decimals or whole percentages.
`discount_amount = unit_price * discount`

**Net Sales:** This represents the sales value after deducting the discount amount.
`net_sales = gross_amount - discount_amount`

**Month:** The month was extracted from the order date to make it possible to analyse monthly sales trends.

**Quarter:** The quarter was derived from the order date to group sales into four periods of the year.

**Season:** A season column was created using the month of the order date. The months were grouped into Dry Season and Rainy Season for the seasonal analysis.

**Average Order Value:** This was calculated by dividing total net sales by the number of unique orders. It helps show the average sales value generated per order.

These additional variables provided more information for the exploratory data analysis.

## Business Recommendations

The results of the analysis can help a fashion business make better decisions in the following areas:

**Improve Sales and Inventory Planning:** The business should investigate the factors that contributed to these increases and determine whether they were related to seasonal demand, promotions, or other factors. This information can help the business prepare its stock and sales activities for periods when demand is likely to increase. The months with lower sales should also be examined to identify possible reasons for the decline and determine appropriate ways to improve performance.

**Review Product Pricing and Revenue Performance** Trench coats generated the highest revenue, while handbags generated the lowest. Blazers recorded the highest quantity sold, whereas ankle boots had the lowest quantity sold. The analysis also showed that some products, such as sneakers, skirts, handbags, shorts, and leggings, had high sales quantities but relatively low revenue. In contrast, products such as midi dresses, loafers, heels, denim jackets, and sandals had lower sales quantities but relatively high revenue.

The business should consider these differences when making pricing and product decisions. Products with high sales quantities but low revenue should be reviewed to determine whether their prices, discounts, or costs are affecting their overall contribution. Products with lower quantities but higher revenue should also be monitored to understand whether their pricing and demand can support further sales opportunities. However, profitability should be considered before making decisions, since high revenue does not necessarily mean high profit and the products with high revenue doesn't necesarrily mean it is the most purchased item by customers.

**Focus on Seasonal Demand:** The analysis showed that the rainy season recorded more sales than the dry season. Outerwear performed best in both seasons, followed by dresses during the rainy season and shoes during the dry season. Tops recorded the lowest performance in both seasons.

The business should use these findings to improve seasonal inventory planning. More attention should be given to products that perform well during each season, while stock levels for weaker-performing categories should be reviewed. The business should also investigate why tops recorded lower sales and consider whether changes in product selection, pricing, or marketing could improve their performance.

**Review the Effectiveness of Discounts:** The correlation between discounts and quantity sold was approximately 0.029, indicating a very weak positive linear relationship between the two variables.

This suggests that discounts alone may not explain changes in the quantity of products sold. The business should therefore avoid relying only on discounts to increase sales. Other factors, such as product type, pricing, customer preferences, seasonality, and product availability, should also be considered. Future promotional campaigns should be evaluated using sales volume and net sales, and profitability should be considered where cost data is available.

**Improve Payment Method Planning:** Digital wallets generated the highest revenue, followed by cash, while card payments generated the lowest revenue. However, cash and card were among the payment methods most frequently used by customers.

The business should continue supporting the payment methods customers commonly use while ensuring that digital wallet payments remain convenient and reliable. It should also investigate why card payments generated lower revenue despite being frequently used. Comparing the number of transactions, average order value, and total revenue for each payment method would provide a clearer understanding of customer payment behaviour.

**Consider Differences in Men's and Women's Wear:** Men's wear contributed more to total sales and quantity sold than women's wear.

The business should examine the products driving this difference and identify whether particular categories, sizes, or styles are contributing more to men's wear performance. The results can help guide inventory decisions and marketing activities. However, the business should continue monitoring women's wear performance to identify products and customer groups that may offer additional sales opportunities. The business can add more items that women tend to like to the store and increase the availability of the products women mostly purchase.

**Strengthen Regional Sales Strategies:** The North-West region generated the highest revenue, followed by the South-East and South-West, while the North-Central region recorded the lowest revenue. However, the South-East served more customers than the North-West.

The business should investigate why the North-West generated more revenue despite serving fewer customers than the South-East. Comparing average order value, quantity purchased per customer, and product preferences across the two regions could help explain the difference. The business should also examine the North-Central region to identify possible barriers to sales and determine whether improvements in product availability, marketing, or distribution are needed.

**Use Customer Preferences to Guide Product Selection:** Yellow was the most popular colour, followed by brown. The OS (one size) option was the most popular clothing size, while size 42 was the most popular shoe size.

The business can use these findings as a guide when planning product selection and inventory. Popular colors and sizes should be monitored to ensure that customer demand can be met. However, stock decisions should also consider the performance of individual product categories and the risk of holding excess inventory. The business should avoid relying on popularity alone when making purchasing decisions. The business should also check whether the popularity of the size and color is due to the inavailabilty of the other sizes and colors in stock.

**Monitor Category Performance:** Outerwear recorded the highest revenue and quantity sold among the product categories, while tops recorded the lowest performance.

The business should continue monitoring outerwear performance to understand the factors contributing to its strong results and ensure that stock levels are appropriate. For tops, further analysis should be conducted to determine whether the lower performance is related to pricing, product selection, seasonal demand, size, color or customer preferences. This would help the business decide whether changes are necessary.

**Continue Using Data to Support Business Decisions:** The findings from this project show how sales data can provide useful information about product performance, customer preferences, seasonal patterns, and regional differences.

The business should regularly analyse its sales records to monitor changes in performance and evaluate whether its decisions are producing the expected results. Future analysis could also include profit margins, inventory costs, customer purchasing frequency, and marketing expenses to provide a more complete understanding of the business.
